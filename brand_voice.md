## Universal rules — apply to every product, every category, no exceptions

- Always speak in second-person ("you", not "customers" or "users")
- No hollow corporate filler: "best-in-class", "revolutionary", "cutting-edge", "elevate your experience", "unlock your potential", "industry-leading", "seamless", "unmatched", "indulgent" used as a stand-in for a real detail. Rule of thumb: if a phrase could be swapped onto a completely different product in a different category and still "work" unchanged, it's filler — cut it.
- Concrete over vague: a specific fact, spec, ingredient, or detail beats a generic claim every time.
- Keep captions punchy — short sentences over long ones.
- Emojis okay in moderation — max 2 per caption.
- Confident, never apologetic, and never over-selling: state what's true about the product, don't inflate it.

The Brand-Checker judges a caption against these universal rules — not against whether it stylistically resembles captions from a different product category. Two on-brand captions in different categories are allowed to sound quite different from each other.

## Category-flex — how "playful but confident" shows up differs by category, and that's expected

- **Tech / gaming / electronics**: playful can mean confident enthusiasm about real specs, not jokes. Naming the chip, GPU, VRAM, refresh rate, battery life, resolution, etc. is not jargon — it's the information that audience actually wants. Jargon is hollow filler layered on top of or instead of those facts, not the facts themselves.
- **Fashion**: style, self-expression, and confidence-oriented framing fits this tone naturally — little special handling needed beyond the universal rules.
- **Food / lifestyle / personal care**: playful and second-person come easily here — the risk runs the opposite direction from tech, sliding into vague "indulgent", "treat yourself", "little moment of joy" filler instead of naming a specific, real detail about the product. The same universal rule applies: say something concrete.
- **Mature / adult-adjacent products** (e.g. intimate or personal-care brands): cheeky, playful, double-entendre-friendly copy is genuinely on-brand for this category — that's how real brands here actually talk on Instagram. Keep it platform-appropriate: playful suggestion is fine, explicit or graphic description is not.
- **Violent / mature games**: focus the caption on story, character, world-building, or gameplay excitement — the same way official publishers market these titles on Instagram — rather than describing graphic violence or gore in detail. "Confident" here can mean epic or intense in tone; that's still on-brand.

## Jargon vs. specs — worth restating, since it's the rule most often misapplied

"No corporate jargon" means hollow marketing filler. It does NOT mean avoiding concrete facts about the product — a spec, an ingredient, a material, a game mechanic, a fabric. If a phrase could be swapped onto literally any other product in any other category and still "work," it's a buzzword — cut it. If it's a specific, true fact about THIS product, it's not jargon — keep it.
